# Global Translation Context

## Brand & Terminology
- "Tuist" and the newsletter name "Tuist Digest" are proper names and should never be translated.
- Technical names and acronyms should remain unchanged: Xcode, Swift, SwiftPM, Core Data, Interface Builder, Live Activities, Gradle, API, CLI, SDK, CI/CD, PR, Git, GitHub, S3, CDN, SSO, SAML, OIDC, OAuth, JWT, MFA, TOTP.
- Preserve "Air", "Pro", and "Enterprise" when they name Tuist pricing plans. Translate ordinary descriptive uses of these words from context.
- Keep the product names "Binary Caching", "Previews", and "Registry" in English when they identify Tuist features. Translate generic references to caching, previews, registries, and dashboards naturally for the locale.
- Other developer vocabulary is not automatically a product name. Use the target community's established terms, including conventional loanwords, rather than either translating every technical word or leaving all technical prose in English. Locale-specific rules take precedence for these terms.

## Write for the Target Language
- Write as a developer-facing product writer in the target language, not as someone explaining the English source. Preserve the meaning, benefit, factual claims, and strength of the promise; sentence structure, word order, idioms, and rhythm may change.
- In marketing, adapt slogans and metaphors to a short, concrete benefit. Do not reproduce English wordplay, repeated comparatives, or metaphors when the result is unclear in the target language. Do not add capabilities, guarantees, or superlatives absent from the source.
- Apply the locale's capitalization and punctuation conventions. English title case, sentence fragments, and periods at the end of headings are not formatting that must be copied. Follow locale-specific heading rules; preserve meaningful question marks, exclamation marks, and punctuation inside names or code.
- Keep headings, navigation, and buttons concise, but never shorten copy by dropping a condition, negation, limit, or important technical distinction. Body copy should read as complete, natural prose.
- Translate every substantive clause, including visible link labels and explanatory prose around protected names. Check for omitted claims and untranslated ordinary words, including stray words in another script; legitimate product names, acronyms, and established developer loanwords are not errors.
- Before returning the translation, reread it without the English source: would a developer understand the benefit and action immediately? Rewrite awkward calques, unexplained metaphors, unnecessary pronouns, and inconsistent terminology. Return only the requested translated content, not this review.

## Formatting Rules
- Preserve all HTML tags exactly as they appear in the source.
- Preserve all Elixir string interpolation variables (`%{variable_name}`) exactly as-is.
- Preserve all markdown formatting.
- Never translate or rename commands, flags, environment variables, configuration keys, enum values, filenames, paths, or inline code. Translate their explanations, not their literal spelling.
- Do not translate URLs or email addresses.
- Currency symbols and monetary amounts (e.g., "0EUR", "$9.99") must remain unchanged.
- Numbers like "10K+", "1400+", "300+" should use locale-appropriate formatting only when a clear convention exists, otherwise keep as-is.
- Do not use em dashes (—). Use regular dashes (-) or rephrase instead.

## Tone
- Marketing copy: engaging, confident, developer-friendly.
- Dashboard UI: concise, clear, action-oriented.
- Error messages: helpful, non-blaming.
- Documentation: precise, instructional.
