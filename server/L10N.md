# Tuist Server Translation Context

The Tuist server is a web application providing a dashboard, marketing website, and documentation for Tuist -- a tool for managing and scaling Xcode projects and beyond.

## Audience and Meaning

- Write for software developers and platform teams working with Xcode, Swift, Gradle, local development, CI, and AI coding agents. Assume technical literacy, not familiarity with English marketing idioms.
- "Ship" and "shipping" mean delivering working software or updates to users, not freight, sending files, or announcing a launch. In slogans, the benefit is less time waiting on tooling and more time delivering useful changes. Use a natural local expression or an established developer loanword, following the locale guide.
- "More in the toolbox" introduces additional Tuist features. It does not describe a physical box or a separate toolbox product. Convey that there are more useful tools or capabilities for the team, without preserving the metaphor at the expense of clarity.
- A "build" can be the compilation process, one execution, or the resulting app artifact. A "run" is an execution of a command, build, or test, not physical movement. A "runner" is a machine or execution agent, not a person running. A "target" is a build-system unit, not a generic goal. Choose the term from context; do not turn "builds" into a count unless it labels a count.
- "Package resolution" means resolving software dependencies, not resolving a dispute. "Agentic environments" are environments used by AI coding agents, not sales agents. A "feedback loop" is the repeated edit, build, test, and inspect cycle.
- Billing "seats" are user licenses, not chairs. "Egress" is data downloaded from Tuist's caches, measured in bytes, not the number of downloads. A request counts a download. Preserve allowances, billing conditions, units, and the distinction between free and billed usage.
- In usage summaries, "Last period came to %{count} minutes" reports consumed runner minutes, not the duration of the billing period. "On track for" introduces an estimated usage total, not a target or guarantee. Traffic that "counts at half" consumes half the normal allowance; do not introduce a half-price claim unless the source explicitly describes pricing.
- "Minimal overhead" describes additional runtime or resource cost, not a small payment. A build artifact is software output, not an archaeological object or a construction material.
- Newsletter "issues" are editions; GitHub "issues" are tracked reports or tasks. "Imprint" links to legal company information, not printing. "All systems normal" describes service availability, not operating systems.
- Use the translation domain, source-reference comments, and related entries to interpret a string. Headings, buttons, metric labels, descriptions, and customer quotes need different sentence forms.

## Marketing Copy and Split Strings

- Translate the message and benefit, not each English word. Prefer concrete descriptions of saved time, faster feedback, and simpler workflows over abstract or literal slogans.
- The home-page hero consists of two separate strings, "Less waiting." and "More shipping.". Translate them as two complementary headline lines, not unrelated sentences or two copies of the whole slogan.
- Other split headings include "Never build the" + "same thing twice.", "Stop waiting on the" + "queue.", and "Faster tests you can" + "actually trust.". Make the combined heading natural and grammatical; each translation must contain only its part of the message. Do not translate a fragment as a standalone sentence.
- The download-page sequence "Brings your latest previews together," + "lets you inspect them in detail," + "and runs them directly on your device" shares the same object. Keep pronouns and grammatical gender consistent across the sequence.
- Treat examples in locale guides as editorial direction, not mandatory replacements or new product claims. Adapt them to the actual source. Do not insert a slogan into dashboard labels or technical documentation, or rewrite customer quotes into stronger endorsements.
- Keep technical terms and localized feature names consistent between navigation, feature cards, page headings, and descriptions. Glossary wording may inflect and change capitalization to fit the sentence; it is not a mechanical replacement rule. Code and interpolation tokens must never inflect.
- Follow the target language's heading punctuation and capitalization conventions. Keep normal sentence punctuation in paragraphs, descriptions, and quotations.

## Metrics and Test States

- Cache hits and misses describe reuse, not success and failure. A task miss means the task was not reused from cache, not that execution failed. In selective testing, hits count modules skipped through reuse; misses count modules that had to run.
- Cache miss reasons are distinct: "changed" means the module's own inputs changed; "upstream" means dependencies changed; "cold" means insufficient prior evidence; "evicted" means a previously served key is now missing. Preserve the reason in compact labels instead of translating it as a changed failure, skipped work, or lost cache data.
- A flaky test is intermittently unreliable; it is not simply a slow test or any failed test. "Muted" tests still execute but their failures are ignored. "Skipped" tests do not execute. "Quarantined" includes muted and skipped tests; do not merge these states into one translation.
- Distinguish actions, current states, and completed events. "Unmark as flaky" removes an existing classification; it does not mean "do not mark". "Quarantined by" identifies an actor; "Quarantined at" identifies a timestamp.
- Distinguish unique test cases, individual case executions, and suite-level test runs. Preserve whether a metric counts unique cases once or counts every execution.
- In threshold rules, "over" a window means measured during that period, not greater than the period. Compare the candidate build or bundle against the baseline; do not say the baseline itself changed.
- Preserve statistical labels such as `p50`, `p90`, and `p99`, comparison operators, percentages, units, and placeholder names. A percentile is not a percentage or an average.

## Translation Domains

- **marketing**: Public-facing marketing website. Tone should be engaging and persuasive.
- **dashboard**: Main application interface for logged-in users. Tone should be concise and clear.
- **dashboard_account**: Account settings, billing, and organization management.
- **dashboard_auth**: Authentication flows (login, signup, password reset).
- **dashboard_builds**: Build analytics and insights.
- **dashboard_cache**: Binary caching features.
- **dashboard_integrations**: Third-party integrations (GitHub, Slack, etc.).
- **dashboard_previews**: App preview deployment features.
- **dashboard_projects**: Project management features.
- **dashboard_runners**: CI runners, jobs, and cache volumes.
- **dashboard_tests**: Test analytics and reporting.
- **dashboard_usage**: Usage and billing analytics.
- **dashboard_gradle**: Gradle build integration.
- **dashboard_slack**: Slack integration.
- **docs**: API documentation pages.
- **errors**: Error messages shown across the application.
- **default**: General-purpose translations used across all areas.

The source glob covers every Gettext domain. Targets include every existing non-English catalog locale, including locales not currently offered in the language picker. English `.po` files are source-language catalogs, not translation targets.

## Plural Forms by Locale

When translating plural forms, use the correct number of plural forms for the target locale:
- Arabic (ar): 6 plural forms
- Spanish (es), Portuguese (pt), Turkish (tr): 2 plural forms
- Georgian (ka): 2 plural forms
- Japanese (ja), Korean (ko), Chinese (zh_Hans, zh_Hant), Cantonese (yue_Hant): 1 plural form (no distinction)
- Polish (pl), Russian (ru): 3 plural forms
