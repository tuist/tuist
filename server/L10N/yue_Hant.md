## Cantonese-specific translation rules

## Voice and Typography

- Write Cantonese in Traditional Chinese characters for developers. This is a distinct language target, not Mandarin with character conversion; the locale does not prescribe a country or region.
- In marketing, use natural, restrained Cantonese forms such as "你", "我哋", "嘅", and "同" where they belong. Avoid both Mandarin sentence patterns and exaggerated slang. Dashboard labels can be more compact and neutral; do not insert conversational particles into every label.
- Omit final "。" or ASCII periods in headings, navigation, buttons, and short feature labels. Use normal Chinese punctuation in descriptions and paragraphs. Do not insert spaces between Chinese words; keep Latin product names and acronyms readable.

## Developer Vocabulary and Marketing

- Use consistent technical vocabulary, including the shared Traditional Chinese labels in the glossary. Established English loanwords can fit conversational copy, but do not alternate equivalent labels arbitrarily or assume every English technical word is a protected name.
- "Shipping" means releasing or delivering software updates, not "送貨" or "出貨". Prefer a direct benefit over a literal toolbox metaphor.
- For "Less waiting. More shipping.", a natural direction is "等少啲，專心開發多啲". For "More in the toolbox", prefer "更多工具，幫你同團隊開發".
- A newsletter issue is a "期", not a problem or bug. In cache and test summaries, distinguish reused work, executed work, failures, and skipped tests; do not translate every miss as an execution failure.
