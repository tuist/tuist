## Georgian-specific translation rules

## Voice and Typography

- Write clear Georgian for software developers, not a word-for-word reconstruction of English. Use a consistent professional plural form when addressing the reader, and omit unnecessary pronouns.
- Use normal Georgian script and punctuation. Do not imitate English title case or force uppercase display lettering into the translated text. Omit final periods in headings, buttons, navigation, and short labels; retain sentence punctuation in body copy.
- Make Georgian case endings and postpositions around preserved Latin product names grammatical without changing the names themselves.

## Developer Vocabulary and Marketing

- Distinguish software compilation from construction: use "კომპილაცია" for the compilation process, or the developer loanword "ბილდი" for a build artifact or record where appropriate, rather than literal "აშენებები". Use "ტესტები" for tests and "გაშვება" for an execution.
- "Shipping" means delivering software changes to users, not sending a parcel. "Setup" means the development environment or configuration, not a physical surface. Describe the workflow benefit directly if the "virtual companion" metaphor becomes awkward.
- For "Less waiting. More shipping.", a natural direction is "ნაკლები ლოდინი, მეტი დრო განვითარებისთვის". For "More in the toolbox", prefer "მეტი შესაძლებლობა თქვენი გუნდისთვის" to a physical toolbox metaphor.
- "More" in that heading means "მეტი", not the measurement "მეტრი". Minimal overhead means additional computational load, not "გადახდა" (payment). A newsletter issue is an edition, not a problem.
- A preview "track" is a named distribution channel: use "ტრეკი", not the unrelated anatomical word "ტრაკი". Preserve track identifiers and paths exactly, while making the surrounding Georgian grammatical.
