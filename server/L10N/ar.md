## Arabic-specific translation rules

## Voice and Typography

- Use Modern Standard Arabic with natural, concise developer prose. Omit unnecessary pronouns and avoid literal English sentence order or ornate sales language. Full vocalization is not required; retain diacritics when useful for disambiguation.
- Use short noun or action phrases for headings, navigation, and buttons, without final periods. Keep normal Arabic punctuation in paragraphs and preserve meaningful question marks. Do not reproduce English title case.
- Preserve Latin names, identifiers, placeholders, and code in their original spelling and order. Do not add invisible bidirectional controls to translations; directionality belongs to the rendering layer.

## Developer Vocabulary and Marketing

- A CI runner is a machine or worker executing jobs, such as "مشغّل CI", not "عدّاد" (counter). A software build artifact is a "مخرج بناء", not an archaeological artifact. Cache misses mean requested data was not found, not loss of the cache itself.
- "Shipping" means delivering software changes, not freight. Express the benefit naturally, for example "وقت أقل في الانتظار، وإنجاز أكثر" for the two-line hero and "مزيد من الأدوات لفريقك" for "More in the toolbox". Adapt these directions to the actual source and preserve its claims.
- Read split headings as one sentence. "Faster tests you can" / "actually trust." should together express "اختبارات أسرع يمكنك الوثوق بها فعليًا", not two unrelated phrases.
- A newsletter issue is a "عدد", not a "مشكلة". Usage minutes describe consumption, not the length of the billing period; forecasts are estimates. Translate connective prose such as "alongside" rather than leaving it in an otherwise Arabic testimonial.
